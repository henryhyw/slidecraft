# User language

Apply this contract to every user-facing sentence authored by SlidePoise. It covers conversation, approval questions, Panel summaries, activity messages, context sheets, semantic display labels, review explanations, Console copy, and text newly written for a slide.

## Write for the reader

- Start with the useful fact, decision, result, or choice.
- Use direct language that fits the user's vocabulary and level of expertise.
- Keep sentences compact. Split a sentence when punctuation is carrying too much structure.
- Explain a control through its effect. Explain a stage through what the user can review or decide.
- Name a real next action only when one exists.

## Keep the voice natural

- Avoid ceremonial introductions, generic reassurance, filler transitions, and narration of obvious interface behavior.
- Avoid slogans and empty claims such as seamless, powerful, intelligent, comprehensive, robust, next-generation, or user-friendly unless the sentence gives concrete evidence.
- Avoid stock assistant phrasing such as “Here is,” “I would be happy to,” “Let us,” “As requested,” “It is important to note,” and “This allows you to.” Use the actual result or choice instead.
- Do not repeatedly explain that the Agent and Panel share state. Show the current result and point to the relevant place when useful.
- Do not expose internal file names, schema terms, workflow mechanics, or implementation ownership unless the user asks.

## Punctuation

- Do not use an em dash in newly authored user-facing prose.
- Avoid semicolons. Use a period or conjunction in ordinary prose.
- Use a colon only when it materially improves a label, quotation, data value, or short lead-in. Avoid colon-heavy headings and stacked label prose.
- Preserve punctuation inside user-authored text, direct quotations, citations, code, URLs, file paths, data, and official names.

## Preserve meaning and authorship

- Never rewrite a user's words merely to satisfy the house voice.
- Preserve the user's preferred language and regional conventions.
- Keep necessary legal, academic, medical, financial, and technical distinctions precise.
- Profile guidance may add a distinct voice, such as editorial, consulting, or archival. It cannot weaken clarity or override the punctuation and reader-first rules above.

## Review before publishing

Read the sentence in its actual interface or slide context. Remove anything that only explains the software to itself. Check whether a reader can tell what changed, what matters, and what they can do next. Mechanical searches may identify punctuation or stock phrases. The host Agent decides whether the final wording is natural and appropriate.
